Leonardo di ser Piero da Vinci, widely known simply as Leonardo da Vinci, was an Italian polymath of the High Renaissance born out of wedlock on April 15, 1452, to a successful legal notary, Ser Piero, and a peasant girl, Caterina, near the town of Vinci. Receiving a basic education at home, his formal artistic training began in Florence around the age of fifteen when he was apprenticed to the renowned workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, where he learned foundational tasks and eventually painted an angel in Verrocchio's Baptism of Christ before joining the painters' guild in 1472. In 1482, Leonardo left Florence for the ducal court of Milan to serve as a painter, military engineer, and architect under Ludovico Sforza, a period that saw him design military weapons, sketch urban plans, conceptualize flying machines, and create masterpieces like the Vitruvian Man, a study of ideal human proportions, and The Last Supper, a mural celebrated for its mastery of perspective and complex emotion. Returning to Florence in 1500 after the French invasion of Milan, he spent the years between 1503 and 1519 working on the Mona Lisa, renowned for its enigmatic smile and the masterful use of the sfumato blending technique. Later, he lived in Rome working for the Pope before accepting an invitation from King Francis I in 1516 to move to France, where he spent his final years at the Chateau du Clos Luce until his death on May 2, 1519, at the age of 67. Beyond his artistic masterpieces, Leonardo left a profound scientific legacy in the form of over 10,000 pages of notebooks written in his famous mirror script, containing rigorous observations, tests, and illustrations on anatomy, mechanics, hydraulics, botany, optics, and geology, including groundbreaking discoveries about the human heart and blood system that presaged modern scientific practices.
